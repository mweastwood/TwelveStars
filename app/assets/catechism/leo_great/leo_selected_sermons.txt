Selected Festal Sermons & Epistles on Church Order
Pope St. Leo the Great
Translated by Charles Lett Feltoe (1894)

Chapter 1
Sermon XXI: On the Feast of the Nativity, I
1:1 Our Saviour, dearly-beloved, was born to-day: let us be glad. For there is no proper place for sadness, when we keep the birthday of the Life, which destroys the fear of death and brings to us the joy of promised eternity.
1:2 No one is kept away from this happiness; all have one common ground for joy: our Lord, the destroyer of sin and death, finding no one free from bondage, came to free us all.
1:3 Let the saint exult in that he draws near to victory; let the sinner be glad in that he is invited to pardon; let the Gentile take courage in that he is called to life.
1:4 For the Son of God in the fullness of the time which the inscrutable depth of the divine counsel had determined on, took on Him the nature of man, thereby to reconcile it to its Author: in order that the devil, the inventor of death, might be met and overcome in that very nature which he had overcome.
1:5 And in this conflict, undertaken for us, the fight was fought on great and wondrous principles of fairness; for the Almighty Lord does battle with His savage foe not in His own majesty, but in our humility, confronting him with the same form and the same nature, a partaker indeed in our mortality, but utterly free from all sin.
1:6 Christian, acknowledge thy dignity, and being made a partner in the divine nature, refuse to return to the old baseness by degenerate conduct. Remember the Head and the Body of which thou art a member. Recollect that thou wast rescued from the power of darkness and brought into God's light and kingdom.

Chapter 2
Sermon XXII: On the Feast of the Nativity, II
2:1 In celebrating the mystery of our Lord's Nativity, dearly-beloved, we must elevate the eyes of our minds above the visible world to contemplate the wondrous works of divine compassion.
2:2 The loving-kindness of the Holy Trinity determined that when the human race had fallen through the deception of the serpent, God the Son should take upon Him the nature of the fallen race to redeem it.
2:3 In this wondrous dispensation, mercy and truth have met together, righteousness and peace have kissed each other.
2:4 For the Creator of the universe did not disdain to assume the weakness of our flesh, taking what was ours without losing what was His own.
2:5 The angelic host proclaimed peace to men of good will; for through the birth of the Saviour, the reconciliation between God and man has been accomplished.
2:6 Let us then walk in the newness of life, putting off the old man with his deeds, and shining as lights in the world through faith and charity.

Chapter 3
Sermon XXXI: On the Feast of the Epiphany, I
3:1 Rejoice in the Lord, dearly-beloved, and again I say, rejoice: for but a few days after the solemnity of Christ's Nativity, the feast of His Epiphany shines upon us.
3:2 On this day the manifestation of the Lord Jesus Christ was made known to the Gentiles, when the wise men from the East were led by a star to worship the newborn King.
3:3 That star which appeared in the heavens was a herald of the true Light which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.
3:4 In the person of the Magi all nations adore the Author of the universe; and God is known, not in Judaea alone, but in all the earth.
3:5 They opened their treasures and presented to Him mystic gifts: gold for the King, frankincense for God, and myrrh for His mortal humanity.
3:6 Let us also offer to our Lord the gold of wisdom, the incense of devout prayer, and the myrrh of mortification and bodily purity.

Chapter 4
Sermon XXXIX: On Lent, I
4:1 While at all times, dearly-beloved, the minds of Christians ought to be intent on spiritual exercises, yet now at the return of the holy season of Lent, we must apply ourselves with greater devotion.
4:2 The forty days' fast was sanctified by the example of Moses, of Elijah, and of the Lord Jesus Christ Himself in the wilderness.
4:3 But the bodily abstinence from food must be accompanied by the spiritual fasting of the soul from all vices and sinful desires.
4:4 Let anger be restrained, let hatred be extinguished, and let forgiveness be extended to all who have offended us.
4:5 Let almsgiving abound, so that the needs of the poor may be supplied and our prayers may ascend before the throne of God.
4:6 For he who shows mercy to the needy will himself obtain mercy from the righteous Judge on the day of retribution.

Chapter 5
Sermon LI: On the Transfiguration
5:1 The holy Gospel which has been read, dearly-beloved, invites our contemplation to the high mountain where our Lord revealed His glory to His chosen disciples.
5:2 He took Peter, James, and John into a high mountain apart, and was transfigured before them: His face shone as the sun, and His garments became white as light.
5:3 In this wondrous revelation, the Lord showed the glory of His future kingdom and confirmed the faith of the Apostles before His Passion.
5:4 Moses and Elijah appeared talking with Him, that the witness of the Law and the Prophets might agree with the truth of the Gospel.
5:5 And the voice of the Father sounded from the bright cloud: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye Him."
5:6 By this voice the eternal divinity of the Son is proclaimed, and the whole Church is commanded to listen to His words with obedience and faith.
5:7 Let us not fear the cross or the tribulations of this world, knowing that through suffering we shall attain to the glory of the resurrection.
5:8 For the glory which was revealed on the holy mountain is promised to all who remain faithful to Christ unto the end.

Chapter 6
Sermon LIV: On the Passion, III
6:1 The mystery of the Lord's Passion, dearly-beloved, is the foundation of our hope and the source of our salvation.
6:2 On the sacred wood of the Cross, Christ the true Lamb was offered as a pure victim for the sins of the whole world.
6:3 In His suffering, the humility of the Son of God overcame the pride of the devil, and by dying He conquered death.
6:4 The Cross of Christ is not an occasion of shame, but the standard of victory and the sceptre of divine power.
6:5 When He hung upon the tree, He drew all things to Himself, fulfilling the ancient prophecies and reconciling creation to the Father.
6:6 Let us venerate the saving Cross with faithful hearts, carrying our daily cross in obedience to His commandments.
6:7 For if we suffer with Him, we shall also reign with Him in eternal glory.

Chapter 7
Sermon LXXI: On the Lord's Resurrection, I
7:1 In our solemnity of the Lord's Resurrection, dearly-beloved, the whole world rejoices with spiritual exultation.
7:2 The gates of hell have been broken, the bonds of death dissolved, and Christ has risen triumphant from the tomb.
7:3 In His resurrection, human nature has been restored to immortality and the promise of eternal life has been sealed.
7:4 As in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive: the firstfruits is Christ, and afterwards they that are Christ's.
7:5 Let no Christian despair of the resurrection of the flesh, for the members must follow where the Head has gone before.
7:6 Let us celebrate this feast not with the old leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

Chapter 8
Sermon LXXIII: On the Ascension, I
8:1 The solemnity of this day, dearly-beloved, brings to its completion the mystery of our Lord's earthly presence.
8:2 On this day our Lord Jesus Christ ascended into heaven and sat down at the right hand of the Father in the glory of His divinity.
8:3 He did not leave us orphaned, but by His ascension elevated our human nature above all angelic powers and principalities.
8:4 For where the Head has ascended in glory, thither the hope of the Body is called to follow.
8:5 The holy Apostles, who had sorrowed at His death, were filled with great joy when they beheld Him ascending to heaven.
8:6 Let us also lift up our hearts to heavenly desires, that while we dwell in this mortal life, our conversation may already be in heaven.

Chapter 9
Sermon III: On the Anniversary of His Consecration, III
9:1 Although our unworthiness might cause us to shrink from so great an office, yet the grace of God and the merit of blessed Peter sustain our weakness.
9:2 For the solid firmness of that Rock which Christ Himself laid does not fail, and the care which Peter received for the universal flock continues in his successors.
9:3 In the whole Church Peter daily says: "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God," and every tongue that confesses the Lord is guided by the instruction of his voice.
9:4 For the dispensation of truth abides, and the blessed Peter, persevering in the strength of the Rock which he received, has not abandoned the helm of the Church.
9:5 He was ordained before the rest in such a manner that from his being called the Rock, from his being pronounced the foundation, he should be the perpetual shepherd of the Lord's sheep.
9:6 Let us therefore give thanks to the Almighty God, who in blessed Peter has provided a never-failing defender and guide for His holy Church.

Chapter 10
Sermon IV: On the Anniversary of His Consecration, IV
10:1 The communion of all the faithful in the unity of the Church is a cause of spiritual joy and thanksgiving to God.
10:2 Though all the bishops of the Church share in the sacerdotal dignity, yet there is a distinction of order and authority established by divine providence.
10:3 Out of the whole world Peter alone is chosen to preside over the calling of all nations, over all the Apostles, and over all the Fathers of the Church.
10:4 Thus, though there are many priests and many shepherds, Peter properly rules all whom Christ also rules as the supreme Shepherd.
10:5 Great and wonderful is the sharing of His own power which the divine condescension bestowed upon this man.
10:6 When we exercise the authority of this See, we attribute nothing to our own merits, but refer all glory to Him whose power works through our ministry.

Chapter 11
Letter IV — To the Bishops of Campania, Picenum, and Tuscany
11:1 The care of all the churches requires our constant vigilance, that the sacred canons of the Fathers may be observed without deviation.
11:2 We have learned that in some of your provinces certain abuses have been permitted in the ordination of clerics and the administration of church property.
11:3 Let no one be promoted to the priesthood who has not passed through the lower ecclesiastical degrees with approved conduct.
11:4 The goods of the Church, which are consecrated to the relief of the poor and the support of the clergy, must be guarded with integrity.
11:5 Let all disputes among the clergy be settled according to the canonical order, avoiding secular tribunals and contentions.
11:6 Maintain brotherly concord and mutual charity, that the Church of God may shine with good order and holiness of life.

Chapter 12
Letter X — To the Bishops of the Province of Vienne
12:1 The unity of the universal Church requires that the ancient customs and rights of the metropolitans be preserved in accordance with the canons.
12:2 The connection of the whole body produces one soundness and one beauty; and this connection requires the concord of all members, but especially the harmony of priests.
12:3 For the Lord willed that the message of salvation should belong to the office of all the Apostles in such a way that He placed it principally in blessed Peter, the highest of the Apostles.
12:4 From him as from the head He willed His gifts to flow into the whole body, so that whoever dares to separate himself from Peter's solid foundation understands that he has no part in the divine mystery.
12:5 Let each bishop, therefore, govern his own diocese within the bounds assigned by the holy Fathers, preserving the peace of the Church.
12:6 We seek not our own advancement, but the defense of canonical order and the welfare of all the churches committed to our pastoral care.
12:7 May the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, uniting us all in one spirit of love and truth.
