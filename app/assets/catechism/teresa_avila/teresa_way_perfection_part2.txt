The Way of Perfection: Vol. II
Camino de Perfección: Contemplation & Meditation on the Lord's Prayer
By St. Teresa of Ávila (Trans. The Benedictines of Stanbrook, 1911)

Chapter 19
The Great Courage and Resolute Determination Needed to Drink from the Fountain of Living Water
19:1 Returning now to what I was saying of those who wish to drink of this water of life: you must understand that great courage and determined resolution are necessary to set out on this path. (Joshua 1:9)
19:2 The devil will not fail to place before your eyes countless fears and obstacles: he will tell you that you are weak, that you will lose your health, that you will fall into delusions, or that you are not suited for so high a prayer.
19:3 But I tell you, daughters, have no fear whatsoever. Place your whole confidence in God, Who never abandons those who seek Him with a sincere heart. (Psalm 9:11)
19:4 It is of the greatest importance that you make a resolute determination never to stop or turn back until you have reached the fountain, come what may: whether you live or die, whether you suffer or rejoice, whether the world crumbles or all hell rises up against you.
19:5 For this water of life is so sovereign and divine that one single drop of it gives more satisfaction to the soul than all the pleasures and riches of the whole world. (John 4:14)

Chapter 20
How Prayer Never Injures Anyone and the True Safety of the Interior Road
20:1 Do not listen to those who say that mental prayer is dangerous for women, or that it leads to illusions and heresies.
20:2 The true road of prayer is the royal highway taught by our Lord Jesus Christ, and it is the safest road in the world. (John 14:6)
20:3 The danger lies not in prayer, but in living without prayer, in pride, and in following our own self-will and disordered appetites.
20:4 If a soul walks in humility, obedience, and detachment, the devil can never deceive it; for where humility reigns, the enemy has no power. (James 4:6-7)
20:5 Let us therefore walk forward with joy and holy courage, knowing that our divine Shepherd goes before us and protects His flock from all wolves. (John 10:11-14)

Chapter 21
The Resolute Determination to Persevere Until Reaching the Living Water
21:1 I repeat that determined resolution is the key that opens the treasury of divine graces.
21:2 He who sets out upon this journey with a half-hearted will easily turns back at the first stone he encounters on the road; but he who is firmly resolved to reach the end overcomes all difficulties with ease.
21:3 Consider how merchants cross stormy seas and endure great hardships for a little perishable gain; how much more should we be willing to suffer for the eternal possession of God Himself! (1 Corinthians 9:25)
21:4 Even if you should die on the way without having attained full contemplation, you will have died in the service of God and in the pursuit of perfection, and your reward will be exceedingly great in Heaven. (Matthew 25:21)
21:5 Therefore, my sisters, let us never faint or grow weary in prayer, for the Lord will come when we least expect Him and will fill our souls with His heavenly blessings. (Habakkuk 2:3)

Chapter 22
Mental Prayer and Vocal Prayer: How True Vocal Prayer Must Be Attentive
22:1 You must know that mental prayer and vocal prayer are closely united, and one cannot be separated from the other without great injury to devotion.
22:2 For that vocal prayer in which a person does not consider with Whom she is speaking, who she is that speaks, and what it is she asks, cannot properly be called prayer at all, however much the lips may move. (Matthew 6:7)
22:3 When we recite the Our Father or the Hail Mary, we must fix our attention upon the meaning of the words and upon the divine Majesty of Him to Whom we speak.
22:4 If a servant speaks to a king with his eyes wandering about and his mind occupied with frivolous trifles, the king will rightly consider himself insulted; how much more reverent should we be when we address the King of Heaven and Earth!
22:5 Let your vocal prayer be accompanied by attention and devotion, and you will find that it easily leads you into the sweetness of mental prayer.

Chapter 23
The Importance of Custody of the Senses and Initial Recollection in Vocal Prayer
23:1 In order to recite vocal prayers with true fruit, it is necessary to practice custody of the senses and withdraw the mind from exterior objects. (Proverbs 4:23)
23:2 Before you begin to pray, pause for a moment to remember in Whose presence you stand, and invite your senses—sight, hearing, and imagination—to retire within the secret chamber of the heart. (Matthew 6:6)
23:3 Just as a wise king builds a fortress and calls all his subjects inside the walls when enemies approach, so must the soul gather all its faculties within itself to converse in peace with its Divine Guest.
23:4 If distractions come, do not be distressed or fight violently against them, but gently recall your thought to the presence of God.
23:5 In this way, by little and little, the soul acquires the habit of recollection and finds great ease and delight in conversing with God.

Chapter 24
How Vocal Prayer Spoken with Attention and Understanding Leads to Contemplation
24:1 Do not think that contemplation is reserved only for those who spend many hours in silent mental prayer; for many souls have been raised to the highest contemplation while reciting vocal prayers with great devotion.
24:2 While a devout soul is saying the Pater Noster with loving attention, our Lord often visits it suddenly, suspending the intellect and filling the will with supernatural light and love.
24:3 When this happens, the soul cannot form words or continue its vocal recitation, because it is entirely absorbed in the contemplation of the Divine Majesty.
24:4 Let the soul then remain quietly in that holy suspension, enjoying the presence of its Beloved, without forcing itself to finish its vocal prayers until the divine visitation has passed. (Song of Solomon 2:7)
24:5 For the end of all prayer is union with God, and when the Lord Himself takes possession of the soul, all human efforts must yield to His divine operation.

Chapter 25
The Great Consolation Found in the First Words of the Lord's Prayer: "Our Father"
25:1 O my Lord Jesus Christ, what a master of prayer Thou art! How full of wisdom, sweetness, and consolation are the words which Thou hast taught us in the Lord's Prayer! (Matthew 6:9)
25:2 When we begin by saying: "Our Father," what immense gratitude and joy should fill our hearts!
25:3 That God, Who is infinite in majesty, power, and holiness, before Whom the Angels tremble in adoration, should permit us, who are miserable sinners, to call Him "Father"! (Romans 8:15)
25:4 In this single word "Our," our Lord teaches us that we are all brothers and sisters, children of the same Father, and that we must love and assist one another with tender fraternal charity.
25:5 He who has God for his Father lacks nothing, and need fear neither poverty, nor persecution, nor death itself, for what can separate us from the love of such a Father? (Romans 8:38-39)

Chapter 26
The Soul Contemplates the Divine Fatherhood and the Excellency of Our Heavenly Father
26:1 If an earthly king were to adopt a poor beggar as his child, what great honor and joy that beggar would feel!
26:2 Yet what is any earthly monarch compared to the King of Kings, Who has adopted us as His true children through the grace of baptism and the precious Blood of His only-begotten Son? (1 John 3:1)
26:3 Consider, daughters, how good, how loving, and how merciful this Father is: He pardons our offenses as soon as we repent; He bears with our weaknesses; He comforts us in our tribulations; and He prepares for us an eternal kingdom in Heaven. (Psalm 102:13)
26:4 When you say "Our Father," dwell lovingly upon this truth, and let your heart be inflamed with filial love and confidence in Him Who created you for His eternal embrace.
26:5 What child would not love such a Father, and desire with all its strength to please Him in all things?

Chapter 27
"Who Art in Heaven": On the Inward Heaven of the Soul and the Prayer of Recollection
27:1 When we add the words "Who art in Heaven," we must not think only of the distant skies above us, but remember that God dwells by His grace in the interior heaven of our own soul. (Luke 17:21; 2 Corinthians 6:16)
27:2 St. Augustine lamented that he had sought God outside of himself, in created things, while all the time God was within him. Let us not make the same mistake.
27:3 The soul in the state of grace is a true heaven and paradise of God, wherein the Most Holy Trinity takes delight. (John 14:23)
27:4 There is no need to have wings to go in search of Him, but only to place ourselves in solitude and look upon Him present within us.
27:5 This is what is called the Prayer of Recollection, which is the beginning of all the supernatural favors that God bestows upon souls.

Chapter 28
How to Practice the Prayer of Recollection and Withdraw the Senses into the Interior Castle
28:1 The Prayer of Recollection is like the retreat of a tortoise or hedgehog into its shell: the soul calls home all its outward senses and faculties, closing the doors against worldly impressions.
28:2 It enters within itself to be alone with its God, Who sits upon the throne of the heart. (Proverbs 23:26)
28:3 In this quiet sanctuary, the soul speaks to Him as to a most loving Father, a brother, a spouse, or a master, without needing loud cries or elaborate phrases.
28:4 Although at first this exercise may require some effort, because the senses are accustomed to wander freely abroad, yet with practice and the assistance of grace it becomes so natural and sweet that the soul would not exchange it for all the delights of earth.
28:5 Let those who wish to learn this prayer practice it faithfully every day, and they will soon experience its admirable fruits.

Chapter 29
The Method of Drawing Near to Christ Our Master Dwelling within Us
29:1 In this interior prayer, nothing is more profitable than to keep Christ our Savior constantly before the eyes of the soul. (Hebrews 12:2)
29:2 Imagine Him standing beside you, or dwelling within you, looking upon you with infinite love and compassion.
29:3 Speak to Him with filial confidence of all your needs, your sorrows, your weaknesses, and your desires.
29:4 If you are in sorrow or suffering, look upon Him in His Agony in the Garden of Gethsemane or carrying His Cross to Calvary; if you are in joy, behold Him in His glorious Resurrection. (Philippians 3:10)
29:5 He is our true Master, our Physician, our Redeemer, and our All; the more closely we remain united to Him, the more rapidly we shall advance along the way of perfection.

Chapter 30
"Hallowed Be Thy Name, Thy Kingdom Come": Contemplating the Sovereign Majesty and the Prayer of Quiet
30:1 Having addressed our Heavenly Father, our Lord teaches us to say: "Hallowed be Thy Name: Thy Kingdom come." (Matthew 6:9-10)
30:2 What kingdom is this that we ask for? It is the kingdom of divine grace within our souls, and ultimately the kingdom of glory in Heaven.
30:3 When the King of Glory establishes His reign in the soul, He bestows upon it that supernatural favor which is called the Prayer of Quiet.
30:4 In this prayer, the will is captivated and absorbed in the love of God, resting in profound peace and sweetness, while the intellect and memory may still experience some distractions.
30:5 The soul tastes a heavenly joy which exceeds all earthly pleasures, and realizes that the Kingdom of God has indeed begun within it. (Romans 14:17)

Chapter 31
On the Prayer of Quiet and the Heavenly Peace Infused into the Soul
31:1 In the Prayer of Quiet, the soul feels an intimate and supernatural peace which it cannot produce by its own efforts, however much it may meditate or strive. (Psalm 45:11)
31:2 It is a gift that comes directly from the Holy Ghost, filling the heart with divine sweetness and silent adoration.
31:3 The soul understands that it is in the immediate presence of its God, and it would not wish to move, or speak, or even breathe, lest it should lose this celestial contentment.
31:4 Let the soul in this state remain quiet and peaceful; let it not weary itself with many thoughts or reasoning, but let the will rest lovingly in God like a child upon the breast of its mother. (Psalm 130:2)
31:5 If the intellect wanders, let the will not leave its divine resting-place to pursue it, for the intellect will eventually return, drawn by the fragrance of divine grace.

Chapter 32
"Thy Will Be Done on Earth as It Is in Heaven": Perfect Resignation and Surrender to God's Will
32:1 The third petition of the Lord's Prayer is: "Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven." (Matthew 6:10)
32:2 This is the highest and most difficult petition of all, for it requires the complete surrender and annihilation of our own will. (Luke 22:42)
32:3 To say these words sincerely means that we accept with equal joy sickness or health, poverty or riches, dishonor or praise, consolations or desolations, life or death, provided only that the will of God be fulfilled in us.
32:4 The Angels and Saints in Heaven have no other desire than the will of God; and we, who are on earth, must strive to imitate their perfect conformity.
32:5 When a soul has arrived at this complete resignation, it has attained the summit of Christian perfection, and nothing in this world can ever disturb its deep interior peace. (Philippians 4:11-13)

Chapter 33
"Give Us This Day Our Daily Bread": The Heavenly Food of the Holy Eucharist
33:1 In the fourth petition, our divine Master bids us ask: "Give us this day our daily bread." (Matthew 6:11; Luke 11:3)
33:2 Although this includes the necessary food and sustenance for our mortal bodies, the primary and sublime meaning of this bread is the Most Blessed Sacrament of the Altar, the Living Bread that came down from Heaven. (John 6:51)
33:3 Our sweet Jesus, knowing our great weakness and the perils of our exile, was not content with giving Himself once upon the Cross for our redemption, but willed to remain with us daily under the Eucharistic veils until the consummation of the world. (Matthew 28:20)
33:4 This divine Bread gives strength to the weak, healing to the sick, light to the blind, and supernatural life to our souls.
33:5 How great should be our gratitude and love for so wonderful an invention of divine mercy!

Chapter 34
The Inestimable Value of the Blessed Sacrament and Holy Communion with the Divine Guest
34:1 Consider, daughters, the immense privilege we enjoy in Holy Communion: we receive within our breast the very same Jesus Who was born in Bethlehem, Who died upon Calvary, and Who now sits in glory at the right hand of the Father. (1 Corinthians 11:23-26)
34:2 When you have received this Divine Guest, do not lose so precious a time by running immediately to external affairs or vocal prayers from books.
34:3 Shut the eyes of your body and open the eyes of your soul to gaze upon the King Who has entered into the poor lodging of your heart.
34:4 He does not come in His open glory and majesty, lest we should be overwhelmed with fear, but under the humble appearances of bread, that we may approach Him with filial confidence and intimate love.
34:5 Pour out your heart before Him, tell Him your sorrows, ask Him for all the graces you need for yourself and the Church, for He has come laden with heavenly gifts to enrich your poverty.

Chapter 35
Holy Recollection and Thanksgiving after Receiving the Most Blessed Sacrament
35:1 The time immediately following Holy Communion is the most precious and favorable moment for spiritual advancement.
35:2 The Sacred Humanity of Christ is truly and substantially present within you, and with Him the whole Divinity. (Colossians 2:9)
35:3 Remain in holy stillness and adoration, uniting your heart to His Sacred Heart, and offering yourself as a living holocaust of love and thanksgiving. (Romans 12:1)
35:4 If you practice this holy thanksgiving with fidelity, you will experience how rapidly the Lord transforms your soul, uprooting your vices and adorning you with His own heavenly virtues.
35:5 Never let tepidity or haste rob you of the fruits of this divine banquet, for one single devout Communion is capable of making a soul rich in sanctity.

Chapter 36
"And Forgive Us Our Trespasses as We Forgive Them That Trespass Against Us"
36:1 In the fifth petition, we say: "And forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors." (Matthew 6:12)
36:2 Notice, my sisters, the condition which our Lord has attached to this petition: God will forgive our sins only in the measure in which we forgive those who have offended us. (Matthew 6:14-15)
36:3 If we harbor any resentment, bitterness, or grudge against a sister or anyone else, our prayer becomes a condemnation of ourselves.
36:4 But what offense could anyone ever commit against us that can be compared with the countless offenses we have committed against the infinite Majesty of God? (Matthew 18:23-35)
36:5 Let us therefore pardon every injury from the bottom of our hearts, not only forgiving our enemies, but praying for them and loving them with sincere charity for the love of Christ. (Luke 6:27-28)

Chapter 37
The Excellence of Forgiving Injuries and How God Pardons the Merciful
37:1 The virtue of forgiving injuries is a true mark of the children of God and the authentic disciples of Jesus Christ. (Matthew 5:44-48)
37:2 When our Lord hung upon the Cross in the midst of excruciating torments, His first prayer was for His executioners: "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do." (Luke 23:34)
37:3 When we forgive those who have wronged us, we imitate this divine example and show that the love of God is truly alive in our hearts.
37:4 The merciful soul will find abundant mercy at the judgment seat of God, for "blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy." (Matthew 5:7)
37:5 Let all grievances be forgotten, all resentments buried, and let brotherly love reign supreme among you always.

Chapter 38
"And Lead Us Not into Temptation": The Subtle Snares of the Devil and False Humility
38:1 The sixth petition of our divine Master is: "And lead us not into temptation." (Matthew 6:13)
38:2 We do not ask to be entirely exempt from temptations, for temptations are necessary for our spiritual testing, purification, and growth in virtue. (James 1:2-4, 12)
38:3 But we ask that God may not permit us to succumb or consent to the snares of the devil, the world, and the flesh. (1 Corinthians 10:13)
38:4 Be especially watchful against the subtle temptations of the enemy that come disguised as virtues: such as false humility, which makes the soul think it is too unworthy to receive Holy Communion or to practice mental prayer.
38:5 True humility always brings peace, light, and confidence in God; while false humility brings agitation, darkness, discouragement, and distrust.

Chapter 39
The Diverse Temptations of the Enemy and How Vigilance and Holy Fear Preserve the Soul
39:1 The devil employs many different artifices to deceive souls that are advancing in prayer. (1 Peter 5:8-9)
39:2 Sometimes he tempts them with indiscreet penances and austerities, that they may ruin their health and become incapable of keeping their rule.
39:3 At other times he tempts them to judge the spiritual state of others, or to desire offices and responsibilities under the pretext of zeal for souls.
39:4 The remedy against all these illusions is continual vigilance, filial fear of God, and total openness and transparency with our spiritual director and superiors. (Proverbs 28:14)
39:5 A soul that conceals nothing and seeks only the glory of God will always discover the snare and escape unharmed from the hunter's net. (Psalm 123:7)

Chapter 40
"But Deliver Us from Evil. Amen": Deliverance from the Evils of This Present Exile
40:1 The final petition of the Lord's Prayer is: "But deliver us from evil. Amen." (Matthew 6:13)
40:2 By this evil, we understand especially the supreme evil of sin, which separates the soul from God, as well as the eternal damnation of hell.
40:3 We also ask to be delivered from all temporal and spiritual evils that might hinder our salvation and our perfect union with God.
40:4 As long as we live in this mortal body, we are surrounded by dangers and miseries; therefore the holy soul sighs for the day of its deliverance, saying with the Apostle: "Unhappy man that I am, who shall deliver me from the body of this death?" (Romans 7:24)
40:5 When we say "Amen," we confirm all these petitions with firm faith, hope, and charity, trusting that our Heavenly Father will graciously hear and grant them for the sake of His beloved Son.

Chapter 41
On the Fear of God and Divine Love as the Two Invincible Protectors of the Spiritual Life
41:1 To conclude this treatise on prayer, I leave you two invincible weapons and guardians of the spiritual life: the holy fear of God and divine charity. (Psalm 110:10; 1 John 4:18)
41:2 Fear of God causes us to flee from all sin, to avoid the occasions of evil, and to walk in holy reverence before His sovereign majesty.
41:3 Divine love makes us run with joy along the path of the commandments and evangelical counsels, turning all labors into sweetness and all sufferings into joy. (Song of Solomon 1:3; Romans 8:35-37)
41:4 These two virtues walk hand in hand: fear preserves love from presumption and laxity, while love elevates fear from slavish dread to filial devotion.
41:5 He who possesses both fear and love walks securely through all the perils of this mortal life and will enter triumphantly into the eternal kingdom of Heaven.

Chapter 42
The Final Blessing, Conformity to the Cross, and Thanksgiving for the Way of Perfection
42:1 Thus, my dearly beloved sisters and daughters in Jesus Christ, I have written down for you according to my small ability this Way of Perfection, which our Lord has taught me through many experiences and tribulations.
42:2 Keep your eyes ever fixed on Christ crucified, for He is our Book, our Light, and our Life. (1 Corinthians 2:2; Galatians 6:14)
42:3 Let us embrace His Cross with generous and steadfast hearts, seeking no other glory than to be faithful brides of so divine a Spouse.
42:4 May the Most Holy Trinity—Father, Son, and Holy Ghost—be praised and blessed forever for all His mercies and graces bestowed upon this poor house of St. Joseph. (Ephesians 3:20-21)
42:5 Pray for me, your unworthy sister and servant, that our Lord may have mercy on my soul and grant us all to be united forever in His heavenly glory. Amen.
