Compendium of Theology: Part II
On Hope (Trans. Cyril Vollert, S.J., 1947)
Translated by Cyril Vollert, S.J. (1947)

Chapter 1
The Theological Virtue of Hope
1:1 Having treated of the virtue of faith, which illumines the intellect concerning divine truths, we must now consider the theological virtue of hope, which elevates the will to aspire to eternal life.
1:2 Hope is a supernatural virtue infused by God, by which we expect with firm confidence eternal happiness and the graces necessary to obtain it, relying on God's omnipotence, goodness, and fidelity.
1:3 Without hope, the human soul would despair in the midst of worldly trials or fall into the sin of presumption by relying upon its own frail powers.
1:4 As the Apostle Paul writes in Romans 8:24: "For we are saved by hope. But hope that is seen, is not hope. For what a man seeth, why doth he hope for?"

Chapter 2
God as the Supreme Object and Goal of Hope
2:1 The primary object of hope is the supreme good, which is God Himself, the ultimate beatitude and reward of the soul.
2:2 For nothing created can satisfy the boundless capacity of the human heart except the infinite God.
2:3 The secondary object of hope includes all temporal and spiritual goods insofar as they are ordained to the attainment of our eternal salvation.
2:4 As Psalm 72:28 declares: "But it is good for me to adhere to my God, to put my hope in the Lord God."

Chapter 3
Prayer as the Essential Expression of Hope
3:1 Prayer is the interpreter and voice of hope; for what a man hopes to receive from God, that he petitions in prayer.
3:2 In prayer, the soul acknowledges its poverty and dependence upon divine mercy, turning with trust to the Father of mercies.
3:3 Our Lord Himself commanded persistent and confident prayer in Luke 11:9: "Ask, and it shall be given you: seek, and you shall find: knock, and it shall be opened to you."
3:4 Perfect prayer must be attentive, devout, humble, and founded upon unwavering confidence in God's promises.

Chapter 4
The Model of Prayer: The Lord's Prayer (Pater Noster)
4:1 Our Savior Jesus Christ taught His disciples the perfect form of prayer in the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13).
4:2 This sublime prayer contains in seven petitions the full order of everything we ought to hope for and desire.
4:3 It is the most excellent of all prayers because of its divine Author, its conciseness, its theological order, and its universal application.
4:4 The first three petitions pertain to the glory and will of God, while the remaining four pertain to our spiritual and bodily necessities.

Chapter 5
The Opening Invocation: Our Father Who Art in Heaven
5:1 The prayer begins with an address that inspires confidence and filial love: "Our Father."
5:2 By calling God our Father, we remember that we are adopted children of God through Christ, and that we must love all our fellow men as brothers.
5:3 The words "Who art in heaven" do not confine God to a physical location, for He is everywhere present, but remind us of His heavenly majesty and our celestial homeland.
5:4 This opening lifts our minds from earthly vanity to heavenly contemplation.

Chapter 6
The First Petition: Hallowed Be Thy Name
6:1 The first petition asks: "Hallowed be Thy name."
6:2 God's name is in itself holy and glorious beyond measure; we pray that His name may be known, revered, honored, and sanctified among all mankind.
6:3 We desire that infidelity, idolatry, and blasphemy may cease, and that all rational creatures may praise the true God in holiness of life.
6:4 As the Prophet Ezekiel wrote in Ezekiel 36:23: "And I will sanctify My great name, which was profaned among the Gentiles."

Chapter 7
The Second Petition: Thy Kingdom Come
7:1 In the second petition we pray: "Thy kingdom come."
7:2 The kingdom of God is threefold: the kingdom of grace in the hearts of the faithful, the kingdom of the Church on earth, and the kingdom of glory in heaven.
7:3 We ask that God's grace may reign supreme within our souls, that the Church may spread throughout the whole earth, and that we may enter into eternal beatitude at the last day.
7:4 As Christ declared in Matthew 6:33: "Seek ye therefore first the kingdom of God, and His justice, and all these things shall be added unto you."

Chapter 8
The Third Petition: Thy Will Be Done on Earth as It Is in Heaven
8:1 In the third petition we pray: "Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven."
8:2 We ask that we may fulfill God's holy commandments on earth with the same promptness, purity, and joy as the angels and saints in heaven.
8:3 We submit our human wills entirely to the divine will in all trials, afflictions, and duties, repeating the words of Christ in the garden of Gethsemane (Luke 22:42): "Not my will, but Thine be done."
8:4 For our ultimate sanctification consists in conforming our will to the holy will of God (1 Thessalonians 4:3).

Chapter 9
The Fourth Petition: Give Us This Day Our Daily Bread
9:1 In the fourth petition we ask: "Give us this day our daily bread."
9:2 This petition encompasses both our bodily sustenance and our supernatural nourishment.
9:3 Under bodily bread we understand the modest necessities of daily life, teaching us moderation and dependence on divine providence without anxious grasping for wealth (Matthew 6:11).
9:4 Under spiritual bread we understand above all the Word of God and the Most Holy Sacrament of the Eucharist, the supersubstantial bread of eternal life (John 6:51).

Chapter 10
The Concluding Petitions: Forgiveness, Deliverance from Temptation, and Final Salvation
10:1 In the fifth petition we pray: "And forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors," acknowledging our daily offenses and committing ourselves to fraternal charity and mercy.
10:2 In the sixth petition we pray: "And lead us not into temptation," asking for divine strength and grace to resist the snares of the devil, the world, and the flesh.
10:3 In the seventh petition we conclude: "But deliver us from evil," pleading for liberation from all sin, sorrow, spiritual harm, and eternal condemnation.
10:4 The "Amen" is our seal of fervent hope, confirming our trust that God in His boundless mercy will hear and fulfill our prayers.
